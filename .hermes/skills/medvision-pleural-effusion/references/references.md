# references.md — Pleural Effusion

## S1 — Fleischner Society 2024

**Title:** Fleischner Society: Glossary of Terms for Thoracic Imaging  
**Authors:** Bankier AA, MacMahon H, Colby T, et al.  
**Journal:** Radiology  
**Year:** 2024  
**PMID:** 38411514  
**PMCID:** PMC10902601  
**DOI:** 10.1148/radiol.232558  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38411514/  
**Use:** Definition and thoracic-imaging semantics.

Facts used:
- Pleural effusion is excessive pleural fluid.
- Small effusions may blunt costophrenic angles and appear meniscus-shaped.
- Larger effusions may appear as homogeneous dependent opacities.
- CT may characterize fluid, loculation and associated pleural changes.

## S2 — British Thoracic Society 2023

**Title:** British Thoracic Society Guideline for pleural disease  
**Authors:** Roberts ME, Rahman NM, Maskell NA, et al.  
**Journal:** Thorax  
**Year:** 2023  
**PMID:** 37433578  
**DOI:** 10.1136/thorax-2022-219784  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37433578/  
**Full text:** https://thorax.bmj.com/content/78/Suppl_3/s1  
**Use:** Current adult pleural-disease guidance.

Facts used:
- Unilateral effusion imaging should be interpreted with clinical history and pleural-fluid characteristics.
- Serum NT-proBNP can support a heart-failure hypothesis but should not be used alone.
- Negative pleural-fluid cytology does not necessarily end investigation when malignancy remains suspected.
- ADA/IFN-gamma have context-dependent roles for tuberculous pleural effusion.
- Pleural-fluid pH is important in suspected pleural infection risk stratification.

## S3 — Porcel et al. 2015

**Title:** The diagnosis of pleural effusions  
**Authors:** Porcel JM, Azzopardi M, Koegelenberg CF, Maldonado F, Rahman NM, Lee YCG  
**Journal:** Expert Review of Respiratory Medicine  
**Year:** 2015  
**PMID:** 26449328  
**DOI:** 10.1586/17476348.2015.1098535  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26449328/  
**Use:** General diagnostic framework.

Facts used:
- Pleural effusions can arise from systemic, inflammatory, infectious and malignant conditions.
- Etiologic diagnosis integrates history, examination, imaging and pleural-fluid analysis.

## S4 — Light 2013

**Title:** The Light criteria: the beginning and why they are useful 40 years later  
**Author:** Richard W Light  
**Journal:** Clinics in Chest Medicine  
**Year:** 2013  
**PMID:** 23411053  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.ccm.2012.11.006  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23411053/  
**Use:** Transudate/exudate biochemical classification and limitations.

Light criteria used by Hermes:
- pleural-fluid/serum protein ratio > 0.5;
- pleural-fluid/serum LDH ratio > 0.6;
- pleural-fluid LDH > two-thirds of the laboratory upper limit of normal serum LDH.

Limitation:
- true transudates can be misclassified as exudates; S4 reports this particularly among patients receiving diuretics.

**Title:** Management of Malignant Pleural Effusions. An Official ATS/STS/STR Clinical Practice Guideline  
**Authors:** Feller-Kopman DJ, Reddy CB, DeCamp MM, et al.  
**Journal:** American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine  
**Year:** 2018  
**PMID:** 30272503  
**DOI:** 10.1164/rccm.201807-1415ST  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30272503/  
**Use:** Malignant pleural effusion branch only.

Scope constraint:
- do not apply this management guideline to an undifferentiated radiographic pleural-effusion finding;
- MedVision v1 uses it only as context and does not issue autonomous treatment orders.
