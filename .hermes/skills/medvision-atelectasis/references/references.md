# references.md — Atelectasis

## Source S1

**Title:** Fleischner Society: Glossary of Terms for Thoracic Imaging  
**Authors:** Bankier AA, MacMahon H, Colby T, et al.  
**Journal:** Radiology  
**Year:** 2024  
**PMID:** 38411514  
**PMCID:** PMC10902601  
**DOI:** 10.1148/radiol.232558  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38411514/  
**Evidence type:** Thoracic imaging terminology review / glossary  
**Primary use:** Definition, mechanisms, standardized imaging terminology and forms.

### Facts used
- Atelectasis is partial or complete lung collapse.
- Mechanisms include compression/loss of negative pleural pressure, resorption after obstruction, and increased alveolar surface tension.
- Atelectatic lung has lower volume and higher attenuation than normal lung.
- Associated imaging changes can include displacement of fissures, bronchi and vessels and hyperinflation of adjacent lobes.
- Standardized forms include lobar, linear, rounded, segmental and subsegmental atelectasis.

## Source S2

**Title:** Acute Lobar Atelectasis  
**Author:** John J Marini  
**Journal:** Chest  
**Year:** 2019  
**PMID:** 30528423  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.chest.2018.11.014  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30528423/  
**Evidence type:** Contemporary review  
**Primary use:** Acute lobar clinical context and consequences.

### Facts used
- Lobar atelectasis is common in acute illness, postoperative care and chronic debility.
- Clinical consequences may be minor or serious.
- Consequence depends on extent, mechanism, vulnerability, abruptness of onset and compensatory reserve.
- Routine chest imaging and bedside ultrasound can support diagnosis.

## Source S3

**DOI:** 10.1016/j.rx.2013.08.003  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24252304/  
**Evidence type:** Radiology review  
**Primary use:** CXR signs, volume loss, CT correlation and obstructive context.

### Facts used
- Lobar atelectasis may indicate important underlying disease.
- Resorption distal to airway obstruction is a common mechanism.
- Chest radiography is useful for diagnosis.
- Correct recognition of volume-loss signs is important.
- CT correlation can help evaluate radiographic findings and obstructive causes.

## Source S4

**Title:** Types and mechanisms of pulmonary atelectasis  
**Authors:** Woodring JH, Reed JC  
**Journal:** Journal of Thoracic Imaging  
**Year:** 1996  
**PMID:** 8820021  
**DOI:** 10.1097/00005382-199621000-00002  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8820021/  
**Evidence type:** Review  
**Primary use:** Classic direct/indirect radiographic signs, mechanism taxonomy and pneumonia guardrail.

### Facts used
- Direct signs include crowded vessels/air bronchograms and displaced fissures.
- Indirect signs include opacification and structural displacement with compensatory hyperexpansion.
- Mechanism categories include resorption, adhesive, passive, compressive, cicatrization and gravity-dependent atelectasis.
- Radiographic atelectasis alone should not establish pneumonia; appropriate clinical and microbiological evidence is required in the diagnostic framework described.

### Age note
This is an older classic review. Use it for stable mechanistic/radiographic concepts and the explicit pneumonia guardrail. Newer sources take precedence if a future source conflicts.

## Source S5

**Title:** Perioperative Pulmonary Atelectasis: Part I. Biology and Mechanisms  
**Authors:** Zeng C, Lagier D, Lee JW, Vidal Melo MF  
**Journal:** Anesthesiology  
**Year:** 2022  
**PMID:** 34499087  
**PMCID:** PMC9869183  
**DOI:** 10.1097/ALN.0000000000003943  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34499087/  
**Evidence type:** Review  
**Primary use:** Perioperative pathophysiology only.

### Facts used
- Perioperative atelectasis is common.
- Collapsing forces and surface tension can overcome lung-expanding forces.
- Atelectasis can impair oxygenation and reduce lung compliance.

### Scope constraint
Do not generalize perioperative intervention recommendations to non-perioperative cases.

- Pulmonary atelectasis is common in surgical patients.
