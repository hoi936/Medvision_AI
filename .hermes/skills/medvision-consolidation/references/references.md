# references.md — Consolidation

## Source S1

**Title:** Fleischner Society: Glossary of Terms for Thoracic Imaging  
**Authors:** Bankier AA, MacMahon H, Colby T, et al.  
**Journal:** Radiology  
**Year:** 2024  
**PMID:** 38411514  
**PMCID:** PMC10902601  
**DOI:** 10.1148/radiol.232558  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38411514/  
**Evidence type:** Thoracic imaging terminology review / glossary  
**Primary use:** Definition and standardized imaging semantics.

### Facts used
- Consolidation is increased attenuation caused by replacement of alveolar air by fluid or other material.
- On radiographs it appears as homogeneous increased attenuation.
- At CT it obscures underlying bronchi and vessels.
- Patent bronchi may produce air bronchograms.
- `Consolidation` is a descriptor and does not specify underlying pathology.

## Source S2

**Title:** Radiographic approach to multifocal consolidation  
**Authors:** Kjeldsberg KM, Oh K, Murray KA, Cannon G  
**Journal:** Seminars in Ultrasound, CT and MRI  
**Year:** 2002  
**PMID:** 12465686  
**DOI:** 10.1016/S0887-2171(02)90018-1  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12465686/  
**Evidence type:** Radiology review  
**Primary use:** Broad differential and acute-vs-chronic framework.

### Facts used
- Multifocal consolidation has numerous causes.
- Acute presentations commonly include edema, pneumonia and hemorrhage among important causes.
- More chronic consolidation can include inflammatory, neoplastic and other processes.
- History, signs, symptoms and imaging characteristics help prioritize differential diagnoses.

### Age note
This is an older review. Use it for broad differential structure only; newer disease-specific evidence takes precedence.

**Authors:** Metlay JP, Waterer GW, Long AC, et al.  
**Journal:** American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine  
**Year:** 2019  
**PMID:** 31573350  
**PMCID:** PMC6812437  
**DOI:** 10.1164/rccm.201908-1581ST  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31573350/  
**Evidence type:** Official clinical practice guideline  
**Primary use:** Pneumonia branch only.

### Scope constraint
This guideline informs reasoning when community-acquired pneumonia is clinically suspected/diagnosed in the appropriate context.

It must NOT be used to infer:

`Consolidation = pneumonia`

or to select antimicrobial therapy autonomously.

## Source S4

**Title:** Pulmonary Edema: A Pictorial Review of Imaging Manifestations and Current Understanding of Mechanisms of Disease  
**Author:** Maria Barile  
**Journal:** European Journal of Radiology Open  
**Year:** 2020  
**PMID:** 33163585  
**PMCID:** PMC7607415  
**DOI:** 10.1016/j.ejro.2020.100274  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33163585/  
**Evidence type:** Imaging review  
**Primary use:** Pulmonary-edema differential branch.

### Facts used
- Pulmonary edema is extravascular accumulation of fluid in pulmonary interstitium/alveoli.
- Hydrostatic, permeability-related and mixed mechanisms exist.
- Imaging manifestations vary.
- Airspace opacification/consolidation can occur.

### Scope constraint
Do not equate consolidation with cardiogenic pulmonary edema without compatible clinical/imaging evidence.

## Source S5

**Title:** A Review of Clinical and Imaging Features of Diffuse Pulmonary Hemorrhage  
**Authors:** Reisman S, Chung M, Bernheim A  
**Journal:** AJR American Journal of Roentgenology  
**Year:** 2021  
**PMID:** 33826359  
**DOI:** 10.2214/AJR.20.23399  
**URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33826359/  
**Evidence type:** Clinical/imaging review  
**Primary use:** Pulmonary-hemorrhage differential branch.

### Facts used
- Diffuse pulmonary hemorrhage can be life threatening.
- Imaging and clinical findings are nonspecific.
- Chest radiography commonly demonstrates alveolar opacification.
- Diagnosis/etiology requires integration of clinical, radiologic, laboratory and pathologic findings.

### Scope constraint
Do not diagnose pulmonary hemorrhage from consolidation alone.
